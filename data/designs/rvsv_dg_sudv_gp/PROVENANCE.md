# rVSV Sudan ebolavirus glycoprotein

The glycoprotein module is a `sudan-ebolavirus-glycoprotein`. It replaces VSV G.

Geisbert and colleagues (Journal of Virology, 2009, https://doi.org/10.1128/jvi.00561-09) state that a recombinant VSV "expresses a single filovirus glycoprotein (GP) in place of the VSV glycoprotein (G)." The same paper names the Sudan vector: "The recombinant VSVs expressing the glycoprotein (GP) of ZEBOV (strain Mayinga) (VSVΔG/ZEBOVGP), the GP of SEBOV (strain Boniface) (VSVΔG/SEBOVGP), or the GP of MARV (strain Musoke) (VSVΔG/MARVGP) were generated using the infectious clone for the VSV Indiana serotype, as described recently (11, 15)."

Geisbert and colleagues (Journal of Virology, 2008, https://doi.org/10.1128/jvi.00456-08) gave that Sudan vector to rhesus macaques after exposure: "Here, we administered a VSV vector expressing the Sudan ebolavirus (SEBOV) glycoprotein to four rhesus macaques shortly after exposure to SEBOV." The result sentence is "All four animals survived SEBOV challenge, while a control animal that received a nonspecific vector developed fulminant SEBOV hemorrhagic fever and succumbed." The paper does not state the immunization route or the challenge dose in those sentences. No glycoprotein sequence is stored.

The 2009 paper also vaccinated macaques with a blend of the Sudan, Zaire, and Marburg vectors and then challenged some of them with Cote d'Ivoire ebolavirus. That blend is not this genome, and that paper does not describe a Cote d'Ivoire glycoprotein vector.
