# rVSV Hendra glycoprotein

VSV G stays in the genome. A `henipavirus-glycoprotein` module is inserted after it, between G and L.

Kurup, Wirblich, Feldmann, and Schnell (Journal of Virology, 2015, https://doi.org/10.1128/jvi.02308-14) built VSV vectors expressing wild-type or codon-optimized Hendra virus glycoprotein. The cloning sentence is "The resulting PCR products were inserted into the XhoI and NheI restriction sites (between the G and L genes) of the VSV vector cVSV-XN (35)." Codon optimization is not a separate architecture.

Mice received the live vectors intranasally: "Five groups of five 6- to 8-week-old Swiss Webster mice were intranasally (i.n.) infected with 105 PFU (VSV) or focus-forming units (FFU) (RABV) of the live viruses diluted in 20 μl phosphate-buffered saline (PBS)." The result sentence is "The live VSV vectors induced more HeV G-specific antibodies as well as higher levels of HeV neutralizing antibodies than the RABV vectors." No Hendra glycoprotein sequence is stored.
