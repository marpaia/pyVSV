# rVSV filovirus glycoprotein

Architecture class only. The VSV glycoprotein part is an antigen of class `filovirus-glycoprotein`. Garbutt and colleagues (Journal of Virology, May 2004, https://doi.org/10.1128/jvi.78.10.5458-5465.2004) built replication-competent rVSV vectors in which a filovirus transmembrane glycoprotein was inserted between the matrix and polymerase genes on a glycoprotein-deleted backbone. The cell tropism followed that glycoprotein.

The mouse protection note is the Zaire Ebola challenge result. The same paper states the challenge: "At 28 days after the initial infection, all of the mice were challenged intraperitoneally with 1,000 50% lethal doses of the mouse-adapted strain of Zaire Ebola virus." The immunization route for those mice is not stated. The result belongs to the preclinical implementation of this design, not to the Marburg or Lassa vectors from the same paper.

A 2005 Nature Medicine paper (https://doi.org/10.1038/nm1258) reports replication-competent rVSV vectors expressing either the Ebola virus glycoprotein or the Marburg virus glycoprotein. The Ebola glycoprotein vector protected nonhuman primates. The paper states the dose as "A single intramuscular injection of the EBOV or MARV vaccine." That result is the `nm-2005` implementation.

Two clinical papers report the same genome in adults. Agnandji and colleagues (New England Journal of Medicine, 2017, https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa1414216) state: "The most common adverse events were injection-site pain, fatigue, myalgia, and headache." Huttner and colleagues (Lancet Infectious Diseases, 2016, https://doi.org/10.1016/s1473-3099(15)00154-1) gave the vaccine intramuscularly into the deltoid. At 3 × 10^5 PFU, early reactogenicity was frequent but mild, and 48 of 51 low-dose recipients were seropositive at day 28.

A 2017 New England Journal of Medicine paper (https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa1414216) reports two phase 1 trials of an attenuated, replication-competent rVSV vaccine expressing the glycoprotein of a Zaire strain of Ebola virus. That trial is the clinical implementation. The abstract does not state a phenotype result, so this record adds no observation for it. No glycoprotein sequence is stored.
