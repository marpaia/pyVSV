# rVSV Zaire ebolavirus glycoprotein, Mayinga

The glycoprotein is Zaire ebolavirus strain Mayinga, full length, and replaces the VSV glycoprotein.

Garbutt and colleagues (Journal of Virology, May 2004, https://doi.org/10.1128/jvi.78.10.5458-5465.2004) state: "The glycoprotein genes were derived from Zaire Ebola virus, strain Mayinga, Lake Victoria marburgvirus (Marburg virus), strain Musoke, and Lassa virus, strain Josiah." The transmembrane glycoproteins replaced the VSV glycoprotein: "The open reading frames encoding the transmembrane glycoproteins of Marburg virus and Zaire Ebola virus (GP) as well as Lassa virus (GPC) were cloned into the XhoI and NheI sites of the modified full-length VSVXN2ΔG vector lacking the VSV G (Fig. 1)."

The mouse protection note is the Zaire Ebola challenge result. The same paper states the challenge: "At 28 days after the initial infection, all of the mice were challenged intraperitoneally with 1,000 50% lethal doses of the mouse-adapted strain of Zaire Ebola virus." The immunization route for those mice is not stated. The abstract states that the rVSV expressing the Zaire Ebola virus transmembrane glycoprotein protected mice from a lethal Zaire Ebola virus challenge. The soluble glycoprotein construct in the same paper is a separate genome.

A 2005 Nature Medicine paper (https://doi.org/10.1038/nm1258) reports replication-competent rVSV vectors expressing the Ebola virus glycoprotein. "A live attenuated recombinant vaccine protected nonhuman primates against Ebola virus." The paper states the dose as "A single intramuscular injection of the EBOV or MARV vaccine." The Ebola arm of that paper is this genome. The host sentence does not name the primate species. No glycoprotein sequence is stored.
