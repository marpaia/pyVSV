# rVSV Marburg glycoprotein

The VSV glycoprotein part is an antigen of class `marburgvirus-glycoprotein`, strain Musoke, full length. Garbutt and colleagues (Journal of Virology, May 2004, https://doi.org/10.1128/jvi.78.10.5458-5465.2004) state: "The glycoprotein genes were derived from Zaire Ebola virus, strain Mayinga, Lake Victoria marburgvirus (Marburg virus), strain Musoke, and Lassa virus, strain Josiah." The transmembrane glycoproteins replaced the VSV glycoprotein: "The open reading frames encoding the transmembrane glycoproteins of Marburg virus and Zaire Ebola virus (GP) as well as Lassa virus (GPC) were cloned into the XhoI and NheI sites of the modified full-length VSVXN2ΔG vector lacking the VSV G (Fig. 1)." That replacement is the preclinical implementation.

The animal-safety note follows the abstract: none of the rVSV vectors, including the Marburg glycoprotein vector, displayed pathogenic potential in animals. The host is `other`, because the abstract does not name the species for that sentence. The mouse protection result in the same paper is the Zaire Ebola glycoprotein vector and is not an observation on this design.

A 2005 Nature Medicine paper (https://doi.org/10.1038/nm1258) reports the same replacement class expressing the Marburg virus glycoprotein. That vector protected nonhuman primates after a single intramuscular injection and is the `nm-2005` implementation. No glycoprotein sequence is stored.
